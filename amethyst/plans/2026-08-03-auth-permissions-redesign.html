00 — Findings

Three problems, in the order they hurt

Everything below traces to one of these. The first two are what you'd notice looking at the screen; the third is what a user would notice, and it is the one that matters most.

1 · The same fact, restated up to four times

On the commonest prompt — sending a DM — the recipient's name appears in the title ("Send your message to Alice?"), again in the purpose label ("Send your private message to:"), again as the avatar row underneath, and a fourth time in the red consequence line ("If you don't, your message to Alice won't be delivered."). Six stacked blocks — icon, title, boilerplate paragraph, relay chip, person row, consequence — to ask one yes/no question.

2 · Four buttons, three of which mean yes

Allow once, Always deliver my messages, Always allow this relay, Block this relay. Two of the yeses have completely different blast radii and nothing on screen says so: one remembers a relay, the other silently switches the global policy to CUSTOM and flips two account-wide toggles on. A dialog button should not rewrite settings the user can't see.

3 · The reason shown is often not the reason

RelayAuthPurposeDeriver re-guesses intent from the raw shape of the filters in flight — authors → reading someone, #e → a room, p-tags on a pending event → notifying someone. Downloading your own replies and zaps is #p = me with no authors, which matches nothing, so the prompt either falls back to the blank "Use this relay" or borrows the "Notify:" label and facepile from unrelated traffic that happens to share the socket.

Worse, engagement queries fetch reactions and zaps by #e against note ids (ReactionsFilterAssembler), which the deriver classifies as READ_VENUE. The prompt then asks "Open 3f8a12c9?" — and rememberVenueLabel get-or-creates a phantom public-chat channel in LocalCache for a note id that was never a room.

The fix is upstream, and it already exists

ExplainedFilter already carries purpose: SubPurpose, accountPubKeys and entityIds on every filter the app opens — NOTIFICATIONS, DIRECT_MESSAGES, ENGAGEMENT, PUBLIC_CHATS and the rest are named at the point they're built. The auth path throws that away and re-infers from tag shape. Reading the purpose the filter already declares removes the entire class of mis-attribution below, and unlocks the two states the prompt currently cannot express at all: your own inbox and this conversation.

P11 · what you actually see
P11

You had opened a thread

ReactionsFilterAssembler fetches likes, zaps and reposts with #e = [noteIds]. The deriver reads any #e filter as a venue, so a note id is presented as a room, named by its first eight hex characters.

There is a side effect too: rememberVenueLabel treats any 64-hex venue id as a NIP-28 channel and calls checkGetOrCreatePublicChatChannel on it — minting a phantom channel in LocalCache for a note that was never a chat, and opening a subscription for its metadata.

  • 10A note id rendered as a room name. "Open" is the wrong verb; there is nothing to open.
  • 11Empty body again — venues never populate the counterparty loop.
04 — The prompt

Proposed: one title, one sentence, two buttons

The title becomes constant and states the only thing that is constant — you are about to identify yourself, and here is as whom. Everything variable collapses into a single sentence that names the relay's refusal and the person or thing it concerns, with the avatar set inline. That one sentence replaces the old title, the purpose label, the avatar row and the red consequence line.

The shape

Relay identity (icon + host, the thing being trusted) · "Log in as @you?" (the disclosure, with the account named for the first time) · one reason sentence (varies by state) · Remember for this relay (a switch, not a third button) · Not now / Log in · Never allow · How Amethyst decides (text links).

Four buttons become two plus a switch. The dialog no longer writes global settings; the link goes to the settings screen where those toggles live and are visible.

P1′

The canonical layout

Alice is named once. The question is asked once. The consequence is inside the reason — "won't accept … from someone it can't identify" already says what happens if you decline, so no separate red line is needed.

  • 1The relay leads, because the relay is the thing being trusted. Its NIP-11 icon is already loaded elsewhere in the app and reused here.
  • 2The account is named — new. This is the actual disclosure, and on a multi-account device it is the one thing the user cannot infer.
  • 3The only variable line. Avatar inline, at text size, so it reads as part of the sentence rather than a separate block to parse.
  • 4Remember becomes a switch, so "allow" and "allow forever" stop competing as two buttons. Default off — a one-time allow stays the cheap answer.
  • 5Destructive and navigational actions demoted to text, out of the tap path. Nothing here rewrites a global setting.

S3

The default screen

Seven descriptive paragraphs stand between the top of the screen and the first relay. Most of them restate their own title — "Never authenticate / Ignore auth challenges from all relays" — and the Custom card describes the toggle group that is already visible underneath it.

  • 1Two headers, When to authenticate and What to log in to, for one decision. The second group only exists as a child of the third card in the first group, and nothing shows that relationship.
  • 2Every switch title is a complete sentence and every description repeats it in longer form. Four of these in a row is the wall of text.
  • 3"Message anyone" buries the one fact that matters — it is off, and that is deliberate — at the end of the fourth line.
  • 4Header says Per-relay overrides, but the list is the union of overrides, recorded rationale and last-used timestamps. Most rows are not overrides — they are a usage log wearing the wrong label.
  • 5The chip reads Allow for both "explicitly allowed" and "allowed by your current policy". The underlying model has three states; the control has two, and the user can't tell which relays would change if they switched policy.
  • 6A bare ✕ next to a red chip reads as "block". It actually forgets both the override and the recorded reason, so the row silently returns the next time the relay is used.

Two states are missing entirely: relays hard-denied by your block list (kind 10006), which outrank every control on this screen; and which account these decisions belong to, on a device where AUTH is answered per account.

The proposed default

Same controls, same behaviour, roughly half the words. Nothing below the fold changed meaning — the lists just stopped claiming to be something they weren't.

  • 1The account is on the bar. Decisions are stored per account, so the screen should say whose it is — and switching accounts should switch the lists.
  • 2Policy cards become a plain radio group. Each description is one clause and adds a fact the title doesn't have; the mode formerly called Custom is named for what it does.
  • 3The group header carries the grammar — "Log in without asking when…" — so each row is a completion, three or four words, no descriptions. The one row with a non-obvious default keeps a single short line.
  • 4Exceptions contains only explicit overrides, so the header is true. A two-way segmented control replaces the ambiguous chip: a relay is here because you put it here, and clearing both segments removes the row.
  • 5Block-list relays get their own locked group. They outrank every control above them and were previously invisible.

09 — Handover

What this needs before it can be built

Ordered by dependency. The first item is the one that makes the copy true.

#ChangeWhere
1 Read the declared purpose instead of re-inferring it. Downcast the filters from activeRequests to ExplainedFilter and map SubPurpose → AuthPurposeKind; fall back to tag-shape inference only for plain Filters. Kills P10 and P11, and the phantom-channel side effect with them. RelayAuthPurposeDeriver.kt
2 Add MY_INBOX and THREAD kinds; make MY_OWN_RELAY reachable from the deriver so its existing string and ledger branch stop being dead. AuthPurpose.kt
3 Guard rememberVenueLabel so only ids that are actually NIP-28 channels get checkGetOrCreatePublicChatChannel — a venue purpose should carry its kind rather than being sniffed from string length. RelayAuthPromptHost.kt
4 Pass the account into the prompt. AuthCoordinator already decides per account and askChoice is reused across them — the dialog needs to name whose identity is at stake, and the shared-answer shortcut needs re-examining once it does. AuthCoordinator.kt · RelayAuthPromptBus.kt
5 Split the settings list at the source: allDecisions() for Exceptions, the blocked-relay list for the locked group, allRationales() + allLastUsed() for the log. RelayAuthSettingsScreen.kt
6 Decide what a 60-second silent DISMISS should look like. Today the prompt vanishes, the event stays pending in the outbox forever, and the user is told nothing — the known limitation in the original plan is now also a UI gap. RelayAuthPromptBus.kt

Nothing here changes the decision model: the precedence ladder in RelayAuthResolver, the per-relay gate, and the four custom toggles all stay exactly as they are. This is a copy and layout proposal on top of them, plus one upstream correction so the copy describes what is actually happening.
