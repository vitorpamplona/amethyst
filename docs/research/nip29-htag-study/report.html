Do NIP-29 group ids collide across relays?

A crawl of 25,419 known relays, 33,547 user group lists and every kind-39000 that could be recovered — resolved by relay signing key rather than by URL, because URLs cannot answer this question.

Yes, but rarely, and almost never by accident. Of 3,962 distinct group ids across 1,271 NIP-29 relay instances, 142 appear on more than one relay and 42 are backed by signature-verified, relay-signed metadata. Every confirmed collision traces to a default id shipped with software, a client-generated id republished to several relays, or a relay that cloned another relay's group — not to two operators independently drawing the same random id.

Why relay URLs can't answer this

The obvious method — ask every relay for kind 39000, group by the d tag — produces nonsense. Three confounders, each of which inflated an earlier pass of this study by an order of magnitude:

Copies travel

Kinds 39000/39001/39002 are ordinary addressable events, so any relay may hold a copy. nos.lol alone returned metadata for 1,116 distinct groups it does not host. Of 1,942 relay URLs that answered a kind-39000 query, only 37 held no kind-1 notes — the rest are general relays mirroring other people's groups.

One relay, many URLs

Path-routed deployments such as wss://groups.0xchat.com/<hash>, plus aliases and explicit ports, mean a naive count of distinct URLs multiplies a single relay instance into dozens.

NIP-11 pubkey lies

On pyramid.fiatjaf.com it is the operator's npub; on groups.0xchat.com it happens to be the relay key. It only becomes usable once cross-checked against what actually signs the group metadata.

The way through is that NIP-29 has the relay sign the group metadata, and that signature survives every mirrored copy. So this study defines a relay instance as the pubkey that signs the kind-39000, and treats URLs as a secondary rendering of those keys. Under that definition the numbers stop moving: 1,271 relay instances, of which 1,082 host exactly one group — the one-relay-per-group pattern that *.communities.buzz.xyz, *.spaces.coracle.social and layer3.news/<path> deployments have made the norm.

Every one of the 664 events behind the tables below was re-fetched, its id recomputed from the serialization and its BIP-340 signature verified. All 664 passed — there is no forged or malformed group metadata anywhere in the corpus.

What the confirmed collisions are

Defaults shipped with software

clan-test carries 44 verified distinct relay keys, every copy holding the identical content {"about":"","name":"test"}. One quickstart default, deployed 44+ times, each instance minting a fresh relay key. Same family: clan-test2, clan-scuffed-crew. Short generic slugs behave alike: general, support, h1, orbee, nostrord.

Relays that cloned a relay

strfry.ymir.cloud runs its own key and has re-signed group metadata for ids that also live on basspistol.org (h1), relay.zap.stream (acleprush) and chat.wisp.talk (zoxqa84y6lv7). The group id survived the copy; the signing key did not.

Client ids replayed to several relays

7c92b9a7956977b7 spans 4 relay keys including groups.0xchat.com and groups.satsdisco.com; 997bcfd35d30dfa3 and ~17 other 16-hex ids do the same. 64 bits do not collide by chance at this scale — the client chose the id and published it more than once.

Owner-namespaced ids

npub1…:groupName prevents collisions between users but not between relays: one owner creating the same-named group on several relays produces the identical id each time. 5 confirmed, 62 across all evidence tiers.

Limitations

  • Only 126 of 1,271 relay instances could be tied to a live URL. The rest are known solely by their signing key, recovered from metadata mirrored onto aggregators; their relays are dead, private, or absent from NIP-66. 31 of the 142 shared ids have at least one resolvable URL.
  • NIP-66 is the discovery ceiling. A NIP-29 relay no monitor has seen and no kind-10009 references is invisible here.
  • .onion and .i2p relays were skipped — no Tor or I2P transport was available to the crawler.
  • Two keys signing one id proves two relay instances, not two operators. A relay that rotated its key, or one operator running several deployments, looks identical from outside.
  • _, the NIP-29 relay-wide group, is declared by 20 different relays in users' kind-10009 lists and is excluded from the tables: it is a collision by design, and no relay publishes a kind-39000 for it.
